## Source vs declaration

OEIS A110835 (page recovered 2026-08-26, `oeis_pages/A110835.html`):

> %N Smallest m > 0 such that there are no primes between n*m and n*(m+1) inclusive.
> %C Sierpinski's conjecture (1958) is precisely that a(n) >= n for all n. - Charles R Greathouse IV, Oct 09 2010

Lean (`FormalConjectures/OEIS/110835.lean` @ `2411d22e`): `a n = sInf {m : ℕ |
m > 0 ∧ ∀ p, p.Prime → ¬(n*m ≤ p ∧ p ≤ n*(m+1))}`, and the research-open
declaration is `∀ n > 0, a n ≥ n`. This matches the OEIS definition and comment
verbatim, including the inclusive endpoints (the page itself notes inclusivity
only affects n=1; the Lean test theorem `a_1 : a 1 = 8` agrees with OEIS's
published a(1)=8 under the inclusive reading).

## Notes

- The junk-value risk of `sInf ∅ = 0` cannot bite: for every n there exists a
  prime-free interval by CRT (choose distinct primes p_j > n, solve
  nm ≡ −j mod p_j for j=1..n−1, take a large solution; endpoints are positive
  multiples of n exceeding n). So the definition is total in reality.
- The underlying question remains open; nothing to sync.
