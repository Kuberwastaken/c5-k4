## Site text (erdosproblems.com/85, fetched 2026-08-26)
Let $n\geq 4$ and $f(n)$ be minimal such that every graph on $n$ vertices with minimal degree
$\geq f(n)$ contains a $C_4$. Is it true that, for all large $n$, $f(n+1)\geq f(n)$?
Context given on site: $f(n)=(1+o(1))\sqrt n$ follows from bounds in #552; "easy to check
$f(4)=2$"; related OEIS A006672 ($R(C_4,K_{1,n})$); OPEN ("cannot be resolved with a finite
computation").

## Lean reading
`Erdos85.f n = sInf {k | ∀ G : SimpleGraph (Fin n), G.minDegree ≥ k → cycleGraph 4 ⊑ G}`
and main theorem `∀ᶠ n, f n ≤ f (n+1)`.
- Faithful to the site's monotonicity question. `⊑` is the *non-induced* subgraph order —
  correct: the site says "contains a $C_4$" (a $K_{2,2}$ anywhere, e.g. inside a diamond).
  An induced-$C_4$ reading would be a different (wrong) problem.
- Degenerate range note: for $n\le 3$ no graph contains a $C_4$, so the set in the sInf is
  $\{k > \Delta_{\max}\}=\{n,n+1,\dots\}$ (vacuous implications above the max possible degree)
  and $f(n)=n$. The site restricts to $n\ge4$; the Lean statement's eventual claim is
  insensitive to this, but any computation must respect it.

## Computation (exact, two independent implementations agreeing)
$f(n)=\max\{\delta(G):G\text{ }C_4\text{-free}\}+1$; C4-as-subgraph tested as "some pair of
vertices has ≥2 common neighbors" (impl 1, adjacency bitmask DFS by vertex addition) and via
networkx common-neighbor counts over direct edge-subset enumeration (impl 2).

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f(n) | 1 | 2 | 3 | **2** | 3 | 3 | 3 |

- Impl 1 reached n=7 (2²¹ graphs) in 4.3 s; impl 2 confirms n≤6 exactly.
- DB-sanity gate: f(4)=2 reproduces the site's stated value exactly; vacuous-range formula
  f(n)=n for n≤3 verified against the sInf semantics; every maximizer witness re-checked
  C4-free by both paths.
- Monotonicity data: on n ∈ [4,7] the sequence 2,3,3,3 is nondecreasing — no early
  counterexample to the conjecture; consistent with eventual monotonicity.
- Bracket: exact f(n) known here only to n=7; 2^28 for n=8 exceeds the budget cap (~hours in
  pure Python). No claim beyond n ≤ 7 is made.
