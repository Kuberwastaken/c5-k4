%N Fibonacci-Collatz sequence: a(1)=1, a(2)=2; for n > 2, let fib = a(n-1) + a(n-2); if fib is odd then a(n) = 3*fib + 1 else a(n) = fib/2.

%C Taking a(1)=1, a(2)=1 leads to the all-ones sequence 1,1,1,1,1,1,... (A000012); similarly a(1)=a(2)=b gives "all b's" sequence b,b,b,b,b,....

%C Apparently, for n > 2, the sequence is periodic modulo 2 with period 3. However, this regularity is disrupted when starting at a(34)=4937737952464 a run of 6 even terms appears.

%C It is easy to prove that all the terms a(n) with n >= 10 are congruent to 7 mod 9.

%C Conjecture: for every k > 0 there is an index m such that all the a(n) with n > m have the same residue mod 3^k. - _Giovanni Resta_, Nov 17 2010

%H N. J. A. Sloane, <a href="/A105801/b105801.txt">Table of n, a(n) for n = 1..3000</a>

%p M:=3000: a:=1: b:=2: lprint(1,1): lprint(2,2): for n from 3 to M do c:=a+b: if (c mod 2) = 0 then d:=c/2 else d:=3*c+1: fi: lprint(n,d): a:=b: b:=d: od: # _N. J. A. Sloane_, Nov 20 2010

%t a[1]=1; a[2]=2; a[n_]:=a[n]=(fib=a[n-1]+a[n-2]; col=If[OddQ[fib],3*fib+1,fib/2]); Table[a[n],{n,30}]
