%N Numerator of zeta(4n)/zeta(2n)^2 (with a(0)=2 instead of -2).

%C zeta(4n)/zeta(2n)^2 is a rational value expressible in term of Bernoulli's numbers (A027641).

%C Conjecture: if an integer n > 1 is odd, then zeta(2n)/zeta(n)^2 is irrational. Cf. W. Kohnen (link) and my conjecture in A348829. - _Thomas Ordowski_, Jan 05 2022

%C Conjecture: (1 - t(n))/(1 + t(n)) = 1/2^n + 1/3^n + 1/5^n + 1/7^n + O(1/11^n), where t(n) = zeta(2n)/zeta(n)^2. Cf. A348829. - _Thomas Ordowski_, Nov 13 2022

%C Adamczewski provides a proof abstract regarding the second conjecture in linked article. - _Michael De Vlieger_, Aug 20 2026

%H Seiichi Manyama, <a href="/A114362/b114362.txt">Table of n, a(n) for n = 0..158</a>

%H Tom Adamczewski, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11941">OEIS Open: How many conjectures can language models turn into theorems?</a>, arXiv:2608.11941 [cs.AI], 2026. See p. 14 (proof, Appendix A).

%H Winfried Kohnen, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02864395">Transcendence conjectures about periods of modular forms and rational structures on spaces of modular forms</a>, Proc. Indian Acad. Sci.-Math. Sci. 99(3) (1989), 231-233.

%H Herbert Wilf, <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/4145136">Problem 11068</a>, The Amer. Math. Monthly 111(3) (2004), p. 259; <a href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/30037619">Think Rationally</a>, Solution to Problem 11068 by Kenneth E. Schilling, ibid., 112(9) (2005), 844-845.

%F Product_{p primes} (p^{2n}-1)/(p^{2n}+1) = zeta(4n)/zeta(2n)^2.

%F For n > 0, a(n) = Numerator((D(n) - N(n)) / (D(n) + N(n))), where N(n) = A348829(n) and D(n) = A348830(n). See my comments and formulas in A348829. - _Thomas Ordowski_, Jan 05 2022

%F From _Amiram Eldar_, Mar 04 2023: (Start)

%F a(n)/A114363(n) = -2*B(4*n)/(binomial(4*n,*2n)*B(2*n)) = -2*(A027641(4*n)/A027642(4*n))/(A000984(2*n)*A027641(2*n)/A027642(2*n)), for n >= 1, where B(n) is the n-th Bernoulli number.

%F A114363(n)/a(n) = Sum_{x in Q+} 1/f(x)^(2*n), for n >= 1, where Q+ is the set of the positive rational numbers, and if x = k/m in lowest terms, then f(x) = k*m (Wilf, 2004). (End)
